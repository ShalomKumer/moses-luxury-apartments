# Iron rules

Rules the user set on 2026-10-15 for every project (the general copy lives in the user-level `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`),
merged on 2026-10-31 with the client checklist the user sent for this site. They cover the law around a public site,
accessibility, security of the server, and the licences of everything we ship. A rule is never dropped: its **status**
changes, not its place here.

This is the engineering side. It is not legal advice: what the law requires of this business is the owner's lawyer's to
say (`docs/roadmap.md`, "Before launch: the owner and the lawyer"). Where a row says "lawyer", the code can do nothing
more until they have.

## How the rules are used

- **A plan names the rules it touches.** Every plan ends with one line: "Iron rules touched: ..." (by id, below) or
  "none". A change that adds a cookie, a third-party script, a stored field, a font, an icon, an image, a form, or text
  shown to other visitors always touches at least one.
- **Before a push**, each rule the plan named is checked, plus `npm run verify` (the licence allow-list and the tests
  named in the rows below run inside it).
- **A gap has a home.** Every row that is not _Met_ points to its stage in `docs/roadmap.md`.
- **Status words:** _Met_ (checked, and how is stated), _Partial_ (some of it), _Gap_ (not done), _Not yet_ (the
  situation that triggers the rule does not exist; the rule says what happens when it does).

## What the site is, in one paragraph

A public site showing the apartments or cabins, their photos and details. **It shows no availability and has no date
search or calendar** (the user's rule, 2026-10-31): the owner keeps the bookings in his own Google Calendars, which only
he sees. Booking is a **WhatsApp link and an email link** with a pre-filled message in which the guest writes the dates
he wants; the site has no form, no sign-in, no database, no server code, and takes no payment (payment and the invoice
are between the guest and the owner). The owner as an "admin who sees availability" would need a sign-in: that is a
separate feature, not built (`docs/roadmap.md`). Details in `docs/architecture.md`. Most rows below are short because
of these choices; each says what changes if one of them does.

## Accessibility

Target: WCAG 2.1 level AA at least; the project aims at **WCAG 2.2 AA** (the user's UI/UX standard of 2026-11-01,
`docs/standards.md` section 8.7), which adds focus not hidden by other content (2.4.11) and a 24 px minimum target
(2.5.8). The Israeli standard the regulations point to (SI 5568, which follows WCAG) and its
scope for this business are the lawyer's to confirm. The site is Hebrew, `lang="he" dir="rtl"`.
